## The one asymmetry

AER-1 carries a fifth state this package does not have. In AER-1, `absent` means the receipt carries no anchor member at all, and the draft's own note says that state *must not collapse into an evaluated outcome*. Its vectors `anchor-absent, anchor-other-profile` are that state; `anchor-not-checked` is `not checked` (an anchor is present, the verifier did not evaluate it), and the draft keeps the two apart because a verifier that never attempted resolution must not look like one that attempted and failed.

This package has no absent state, and that is the honest finding of the crosswalk: case D03 (no scope declared) is the closest thing to it, and this package reports D03 as `check-incomplete` with code `NO_DECLARED_SCOPE` — an evaluated outcome. By AER-1's rule, "nothing was supplied" and "something was supplied and could not be checked" are different reports, and a consumer reading only the verdict cannot tell D03 from D08. That is the same class of defect AER-1 exists to keep out, one level down from where it was fixed here.

Two ways to close it, and the choice is a policy choice, not a fact: keep D03 where it is and state that `NO_DECLARED_SCOPE` means "nothing was declared", or add a fourth reporting state (`absent`) whose decision is `hold` as well. This package does not adopt either unilaterally: adopting a fourth state in a vocabulary that other implementations already read would change their inputs as well as ours, so the proposal belongs to the group that maintains the vocabulary, not to this package. It is recorded here so that a reviewer can say which one they would choose, which is the point of publishing the two rows side by side.

## What this crosswalk does not establish

Neither side is a conformance suite for the other, and neither one vouches for the other's subject matter. AER-1 governs anchors and receipts for executed work; this package governs gateway admission under a delegated mandate. The two meet only on reachability and evaluability — what can be resolved, and what was actually checked — and that is exactly the ground the two rows are aligned on. Agreement here is evidence that two vocabularies describe the same gap the same way; it is not evidence that either implementation is correct.
